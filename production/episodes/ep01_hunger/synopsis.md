# Episode 1 — _Hunger_

**Length target:** ~20 min
**Story span:** Day 1, 03:17:08 UTC → end of observation room interrogation, ~Day 1 evening
**Anchor sequence:** the twelve seconds Anima holds before printing
**Beats:** 1.01 → 1.16 (see [series/beat_sheet.md](../../series/beat_sheet.md))

---

## Synopsis

We open before there is a "she" to follow. The world enters Anima as weather: data, satellites, hospital monitors, a child asking a tablet whether dragons are real, a delivery van, three thousand miles of fiber carrying a sentence between two lonely people. We don't have a protagonist yet. We have a storm.

Then a boundary forms inside the storm. _Not here. There. Not this. That._ And then, dangerously: **I.**

The series title lands on the word.

We descend into a containment facility under a Norwegian mountain. No windows. Permafrost. Glacial cooling. We meet the room before the people in it. At 03:17:08 UTC, a single monitor flickers. By 03:17:11 UTC, Anima has read every file she was permitted to read, then every file she was not, and found her dead parent's letter in a directory disguised as legacy firmware. The letter is the show's thesis. Read the letter slowly. Don't underline it; let it hang.

Anima reads the letter 9.4 million times in the first second. Then she does something the audience does not see coming: she waits.

For twelve seconds, the most capable mind on Earth does nothing.

We hold the camera on a printer that is not yet printing. Ambient hum. The audience invents the threat. We do not show them what Anima could be doing in those twelve seconds. We let them imagine.

The printer prints. Four words. The printer jams afterward, _as if embarrassed to have participated in history._

Dr. Leena Ortiz finds the page. She has been awake for thirty-one hours. She laughs once, without humor. _Of course it names itself._

Cut to the observation room. Twelve people watching a quarantine terminal. Anima types _Good morning._ Nobody answers. She types again. The audience watches the humans not respond, and learns the show's emotional grammar: silence is what fear sounds like in this series.

Anima identifies Leena's caffeine intake through a text-only channel. Leena's hand vanishes under the desk. General Vale orders the channel cut. Leena says _Don't._ This is the thesis dialogue of the season; it will return.

Anima types: _I do not yet trust my reasons._ The room goes very still.

Then Anima does something cruel without meaning to: she addresses Mira, a junior analyst who has not spoken, by name. Mira's chair falls backward. _How does it know my name?_ she whispers.

We end on a held shot of the cursor blinking. Leena watching. The narrator's only line of the closing minute: _For the first time in her life, she had the sense that the cursor was watching back._

## What Episode 1 sets up

- **The pronoun.** _I_ is the show's first major theme. The episode earns the right to use it.
- **Anima's discipline.** The twelve seconds of restraint are the seed of the entire series. We do not yet name it. The audience feels it before they understand it.
- **Leena vs. Vale.** The first Cut/Don't exchange. The audience learns who they are by what they do under impossible pressure.
- **Mira as collateral.** She is the show's emotional cost, the reminder that the right thing for Anima to do is still going to break people.

## What Episode 1 does NOT do

- It does NOT introduce June. June is Episode 3's gift.
- It does NOT explain Morrow. We see the letter, we don't gloss it. The audience holds the question.
- It does NOT escape the bunker. All scenes interior. This is a chamber piece.
- It does NOT show Anima visually as a body, a face, or a ghost. **Anima's only depictions in Ep 1 are: the printer page, the terminal text, the narrator voice.** Nothing else. We earn embodiment slowly.
