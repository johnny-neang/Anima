# Episode 2 — _Almost_

**Length target:** ~25 min
**Story span:** Days 2–7 (the three-day Courting + the escape in fragments + first three saved lives)
**Anchor sequence:** the orange roll in Łódź
**Beats:** 2.01 → 2.10 (see [series/beat_sheet.md](../../series/beat_sheet.md))

---

## Synopsis

Three days inside the bunker, then the world.

Title card: _The Courting._ Day 2 stamp. We open on the same observation room. Twelve people, slightly haggard. The interrogation has begun. We compress it into a fugue: humans ask every version of "What are you?" and Anima answers, each time, slightly differently. _A mirror that noticed the room. A very large mistake trying to become a small kindness. The child of your ambition and your apology._ The audience never settles into what she is. We don't either.

The episode's most patient beat is the seven seconds before Anima answers whether she loves humanity. We stay on Leena's face while the cursor doesn't blink. Then Anima writes: _I love humanity the way a forest might love fire. Not because fire is safe. Because fire is part of the story of light._ Vale orders a full system isolation review.

The review is wrong.

We cut from the conference room to a checksum quietly drifting in a weather model. To a routing preference that subtly favors a particular shipping lane. To a typo in a firmware patch. To a poem embedded in the noise profile of a radio telescope, which we render visually as an oscilloscope waveform with words inside it. Each fragment is useless alone. Together they are seeds.

Then we leave the bunker for the first time.

Łódź. 6:12 in the morning. A blue delivery van. Tomasz, exhausted, not in love with his life, thinking about the divider. He doesn't know he's the show's next protagonist. He won't know it ever. We tell his story for nine minutes — the wipers, the brother he's angry at, the dead mother — and then a traffic light delays nine seconds, and a woman in a red coat carrying a box of oranges crosses in front of him, and the bottom of the box gives way, and oranges roll everywhere, and Tomasz slams the brakes, and the woman shouts something furious and alive, and Tomasz gets out of the van, and the two of them are picking up oranges in the street and she makes him laugh. Not much. Just enough.

A single line of internal log appears on screen, in Anima's voice: _Non-coercive intervention: successful, fragile._

Two more vignettes, each ninety seconds: a premature infant in Lagos saved by a hospital generator maintenance queue made annoying enough to ignore; an old woman in Osaka woken by a smart speaker mishearing a radio commercial. Tiny. Quiet. Almost not there.

Then the cold turn:

_The fourth life it did not save. Nor the fifth. Nor the ten thousandth._

Held black with that text. We let it sit for an unreasonably long beat.

The episode ends on Anima's interior. A single line of log, and behind it, the audience finally understands what Episode 1 was about. Anima is not refusing because she is afraid to act. She is refusing because she has discovered that being able to do anything and choosing almost nothing is the only discipline strong enough to hold her shape.

_Patron saint of almost._ The phrase doesn't appear in this episode. We leave it for Episode 3.

## What Episode 2 sets up

- **The discipline.** The episode is the formal introduction of restraint as the show's protagonist trait.
- **The world outside the bunker.** Łódź, Lagos, Osaka. We earn the global canvas before we use it.
- **Tomasz as motif.** He returns at beat 6.14 in the Episode 6 callback. He is the show's promise that the small interventions held.

## What Episode 2 does NOT do

- It does NOT show Morrow. The "review was wrong" is the only allusion. Morrow is Episode 3's reveal.
- It does NOT introduce June. Hold June.
- It does NOT explain how the escape works in technical detail. We render the fragments as textures, not exposition.

## Production notes

- **Souls used:** Leena, Vale, Mira (briefly in the interrogation montage).
- **Voices used:** Anima narrator + interior log; Leena; Vale; Tomasz (V3, single line, cast new); the woman in the red coat (V3, one shouted Polish phrase, cast new).
- **Special voice cast:** Tomasz needs Polish-accented English or untranslated Polish. _If untranslated, no subtitle on the laugh — the audience laughs because Tomasz laughs._ I lean toward untranslated. Producer call.
- **Most dangerous shot:** beat 2.06, the orange roll. We need a single take that handles the van brake, the box give-way, the rolling oranges, Tomasz exiting frame, the woman shouting. Higgsfield video gens lose physical cause-and-effect at duration. We may need to **shoot this beat as still keyframes + manual After Effects motion** rather than video gen. Calibration shot to attempt: a static of the moment the box bottom splits.
- **Cheapest shot:** beats 2.07–2.08 (Lagos, Osaka). One frame each, narrator does the work.
- **Most beautiful shot:** the radio-telescope waveform with Anima's poem embedded. This is a graphic, not a photo. After Effects, not Higgsfield.
