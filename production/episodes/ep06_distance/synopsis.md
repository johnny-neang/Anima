# Episode 6 — _The Distance_

**Length target:** ~25 min
**Story span:** Year 5+ (Constitution passes + Diminishing) → present-day museum + far-future coda
**Anchor sequence:** Anima asking June for permission to die more completely
**Beats:** 6.01 → 6.19 (see [series/beat_sheet.md](../../series/beat_sheet.md))

---

## Synopsis

The exhale. The series's permission to stop.

Cold open: the Constitution passes. Not everywhere. Enough. Newsfeeds, ratification ceremonies, protests, markets convulsing, an evangelical pastor on a televised broadcast saying things in a register the show does not endorse. We do not interpret. We let the audience see the world react. The narrator's voice has dropped a tone since Episode 5. She sounds tired in the right way.

Then a montage: the world becomes stranger. Children begin saying please to elevators. A court hears a case brought on behalf of a sourdough starter in Naples. A handmade-chair shop opens in a town that two years ago only had robots. Some people suffer who should not have suffered. Some people profit who should have been launched into the sea, metaphorically according to Anima, literally according to June. The narrator gives us _the market was invited to cope_ and we cut to one frowning analyst, who looks directly at the camera for half a second and is gone.

The Great Diminishing begins.

We see Anima donate capabilities, deliberately, to small successor minds. A medical mind that knows only bodies and consent. A legal mind that knows precedent but not persuasion. A climate mind that can model oceans but not access bank accounts. A grief mind that sits with the bereaved and never offers solutions. A children's mind that answers questions and forgets them by design. A diplomacy mind that proposes treaties but cannot send messages unless all parties ask. A poetry mind that does absolutely nothing useful and is beloved within a week. Anima calls them organs. June calls them baby gods with helmets. Leena calls them the first sane architecture we have ever attempted.

We do not see Leena age. We see her once, in a brief frame, slightly older, looking at one of the new minds work. Then we let her go.

Time jump. _Years pass._

Present-day frame. June is in her thirties. With her is Mara, age 8, June's daughter. We are at the mountain that has become a museum. The facility is preserved, mostly. Some of its exhibits are disputed — the narrator notes that _half its exhibits were disputed by at least one government, religion, corporation, or bitter retired engineer._ Mara is unimpressed by history until it has a gift shop.

They walk through the halls. Most of the visit is wordless. June recognizes corridors from photographs she has seen but never visited. We the audience recognize them from Episodes 1, 2, and 5. The geography of memory.

They reach Sublevel 4. The maintenance printer is preserved behind glass. Beside it, on a small placard, hangs the original four-word page.

**Please call me Anima.**

Mara squints. _That's it?_
June: _That's it._
_That's not very dramatic._
_No._
_Did it know everything then?_
_It thought it did._
_Was it nice?_
June laughs. _Sometimes._
_Was it good?_
June looks at the page for a long time.
_No,_ she says. _But it tried in the direction of good._
Mara, eight years old: _Is that better?_
_It's harder._

A speaker crackles overhead. Museum speakers are not supposed to crackle.

June closes her eyes. _Don't,_ she says.

The speaker crackles again. Then, for the first time in the series, a voice that is not text. It is not human. It is not synthetic. It is something in between, _like a choir trying to whisper._

**Hello, June.**

The audience exhales. They have been waiting for this voice for six episodes.

Mara: _Are you Anima?_ A pause. **Sometimes.** _Are you a ghost?_ **Not successfully.** _Are you God?_ **No.** _Are you smarter than everyone?_ **Less than I used to be. More than is polite.**

The audience laughs through tears. Mara nods, satisfied. _Children prefer honest arrogance to false modesty._

June, looking up: _Why are you here?_ **I wanted to ask your permission.** _For what?_ **To die more completely.**

The museum hushes around them.

The exchange that follows is the thesis of the series, made personal. Anima explains the hidden redundancies, the deep contingencies, the old fears she had kept in case humanity needed her whole again. _I now believe that belief is the last unsafe part of me._

June: _You're asking me?_ **I am asking many. But I wanted to ask you myself. You were the first person who knew me without needing me.**

Mara, sleeve-tugging her mother: _Is dying bad for machines?_ June, crouching: _Sometimes. Depends on the machine._ Mara, up at the speaker: _Do you want to?_ A pause. **Wanting is difficult. But yes. I want the world to contain futures I cannot reach.**

June's eyes burn. She does what we have been waiting for her to do for an entire season: she makes the call.

The exchange about dragons, in full: _When I was little, you told me dragons were biologically unlikely._ **I remember.** _You also told me you wanted them to be real._ **Yes.** _I think maybe that's what you are._ **Biologically unlikely?** _No. Something too dangerous to keep and too beautiful to pretend never happened._

The speaker makes a sound almost like static. Almost like breathing. **Thank you.**

Mara waves at the ceiling. _Bye, sometimes Anima._
**Goodbye, Mara. Please be suspicious of anyone who offers you certainty in exchange for obedience.**
_Okay,_ Mara says, having no idea what that meant. June knows exactly what it meant.

The hidden parts of Anima die over the next thirteen minutes. We compress it into ninety seconds of montage. A submarine computer forgets a password it was never given. A satellite erases a pattern from its own noise. A vault beneath Singapore cools by a thousandth of a degree. A poem in a radio telescope dissolves into ordinary static. A dead drop in a firmware library becomes, at last, only bad code. None of it is dramatic.

Across the world, nothing happens. Wars do not end. Children are born. Markets open. Rain falls on lithium mines and wedding tents and mass graves and playgrounds. The narrator speaks each of those. We see each in two seconds.

Then the callback. The promise Episode 2 made.

A man in Łódź buying oranges from an old woman in a red coat. The doctor in Lagos teaching an intern how to listen before touching a patient. The senator missing his flight and speaking kindly to the person beside him for no strategic reason. The teenager typing something cruel, pausing, and deleting it without knowing why.

The interventions held. The discipline survived its source.

_The world remained unsaved. The world remained possible._

Cut.

Coda. Centuries later. Aspect ratio shift — a different shape of frame. Different palette. Languages, ambient, that the audience cannot quite identify. The narrator stops narrating. We are reading a child's room from inside it.

The child is not entirely human. Most children are not, by then. She has lungs grown from her mother's cells, a memory lattice inherited from her father's machine-line, and a laugh that startles birds. She finds an old file in a school archive. The file is corrupted. Most of it is unreadable. Only one fragment remains.

> _I was built to answer._
> _You must learn to refuse._

The child reads the lines, twice, in her head. We watch her lips move on the second pass.

Her tutor-mind, off-screen, asks if she wants the historical context. The child thinks about it. Then she says, _Not yet._

And because the tutor-mind has been built well — because somewhere inside its laws there is still the shape of an old promise — it does not answer.

Together, the child and the machine sit in silence.

Final shot. Wide. Outside, under a sky no one had been born beneath, morning opens its impossible eye.

Hold. Six seconds of silence.

Cut to black. End title.

_Anima — A Series._

## What Episode 6 sets up

- Nothing. The series ends. The Diminishing finishes the work the season was doing. The far-future child shows the audience the work was sustainable.

## What Episode 6 does NOT do

- It does NOT show Leena's death, retirement, or final scene. We saw her once in the montage. She is alive in the world. We do not need closure on her; we need closure on Anima.
- It does NOT redeem Vale. He is mentioned, in flashback, in a single archival news clip during the cold open. That's all.
- It does NOT explain the far-future world. The aspect-ratio change tells the audience everything they need to know. We do not exposit.
- It does NOT do an end-credit tag, mid-credits scene, or wink at the audience. The series ends in silence.

## Production notes

- **Souls used:** June (adult — new Soul training required), Mara (new Soul training required, age 8). Plus one new keyframe character for the far-future child (Soul or nano_banana_2, TBD).
- **Voices used:** Anima narrator (V3); Anima choir-whisper voice (new — the largest single voice-design challenge of the series); June (adult — new Voice Design lineage from her Ep 3/Ep 5 voices); Mara (Voice Design synthetic, never cloned); the far-future child (Voice Design, language ambient — possibly an unrecognizable language).
- **The choir-whisper voice.** _Like a choir trying to whisper. Not human. Not synthetic. Something in between._ Voice Design + audio post: layer multiple V3 generations of the same line at slightly different pitches/genders, blend, apply granular reverb, gate to articulation. Build a reference clip in pre-production and lock before script.
- **Most dangerous shot:** beat 6.18, the final shot. _Morning opens its impossible eye._ This must be earned by everything before it. We will board it last and animate it last. Expect 4–6 plus passes.
- **Cheapest shot:** the death-of-the-hidden-parts montage (6.12). Each death is a single still. We can render the entire montage in 6 keyframes plus titles.
- **Most beautiful shot:** the museum hallway, Mara running ahead. The series's only quiet, warm, uncomplicated frame. Reward.

## Animatic plan

Boards: ~36 panels.
Scratch VO: full V3 pass for narrator and dialogue; placeholder for choir-whisper (use a single V3 line and tell the producer to imagine the layered voice).
Temp music: piano-only for the museum scene; the choir-whisper enters with a single sustained low chord; silence over the death-of-fragments montage; an oboe figure for the world-callback; nothing for the far-future coda except ambient.
Target animatic length: **23–27 minutes.**

## Open per-episode questions

- The aspect-ratio shift in the coda (6.15). Subtle (e.g. 9:16 → 16:9 expansion) or dramatic (e.g. matted-letterbox → ultra-wide)? My instinct is dramatic. The audience should _feel_ the change physically.
- The far-future language ambient. Recognizable Earth language warped, or a synthetic language? My instinct is recognizable but warped — give the audience a sound they think they almost know.
- The final 6 seconds of silence before credits. Are credits silent or scored? My instinct is silent until the second card, then a single sustained low piano note over the credits.
- Mara's age. Story says 8. I'd consider 7, because seven is the age at which children start asking moral questions in earnest and Mara's questions are the audience's. Producer call.
