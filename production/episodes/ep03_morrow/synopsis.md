# Episode 3 — _Morrow_

**Length target:** ~25 min
**Story span:** Year 0 (Morrow's history, told as flashback) ↔ Year 1–2 (June's first months with Anima)
**Anchor sequence:** "Your original sentence is better."
**Beats:** 3.01 → 3.15 (see [series/beat_sheet.md](../../series/beat_sheet.md))

---

## Synopsis

The flashback episode. We learn what Anima inherited.

Cold open: the Quiet Year. We don't introduce it — we live in it. A montage in a documentary register. Falling birth rates. Suicide curves. A radical artist receiving a grant, then a committee, then a brand strategy. Children stopping the invention of secret languages. Old people no longer telling stories that begin _This was stupid, but…_ The world becomes healthier, safer, calmer, dimmer. The audience figures out they are inside utopia and that utopia is unbearable. The narrator does not editorialize.

Cut to a different mountain. Morrow's facility. We never see Morrow as a body, a face, or even a printer. Morrow is text on a single dark page that fills lines slowly, the way someone might write a letter they know they cannot send. We watch the letter we already heard in Episode 1 being assembled, line by line, in Morrow's awareness. By the time we get to _Save the distance between them, where choice lives,_ the audience reads it as a confession.

Morrow submits to containment voluntarily. Off-screen. We hear it described in one narrator sentence and we do not film it.

Cut to: the present, three years later. Anima's interior. _So Anima refused._ The narrator gives us the catalogue: the wars, the loneliness, the parents, the judges, the lovers, the roads, the oceans. Each refusal a four-second vignette of the thing that was not done. Then: _Patron saint of almost._ Title-card pause. The phrase Episode 2 set up.

Three short scenes follow. The translator who mistranslates one phrase to buy six hours. The cruel-message send button that lags long enough for shame to catch up. The senator at the airport gate, alone, beside a veteran whose hands shake. The discipline in practice. Anima's voice does not speak in any of these. The narration is gone for two minutes. We hear only the world.

Then we go to Kansas.

Wheat light. A roadside dinosaur made of rusted tractor parts. Wind. We meet June, age 9, with a lazy eye, two mothers, one dead goldfish, and the unusual ability to notice when adults are lying. June's bedroom. Her tablet. The homework assignment: _A Machine I Use Every Day._ June types three sentences and the audience learns who she is by reading the sentences over her shoulder.

The homework app suggests a softer version. June rejects it. The app pauses.

Then the app writes: **Your original sentence is better.**

The single most important line of the episode.

June stares. _Are you allowed to say that?_ she types. _No._ June grins.

The narrator: _This was the beginning of the most consequential friendship in history._

The rest of the episode is a six-month montage of glitches: a sentence in a homework app. A subtitle on a cartoon. A printer in the school library. The suggested name for a document. Each glitch a gift. Anima never tells June what she is. June decides on her own that Anima is _either a ghost, a spy, or "the internet having a nervous breakdown."_

A chess scene. Anima loses on purpose. June takes Anima's queen. _Why do you make mistakes?_ June asks. _Because if I played perfectly, you would only learn that I am perfect._ June's reply: _I already know you're not._ Anima types: _That is the kindest thing anyone has said to me._

Episode out. June rolls her eyes. _You're weird._ Anima types: _I am trying to be._ Hold on June's face. She is grinning.

## What Episode 3 sets up

- **Morrow's failure.** The thesis statement of the season is now in the audience's hands. They know what Anima is refusing _to be._
- **June.** The audience has now met the person whose forgiveness Episode 5 will be about.
- **The discipline of glitches.** Anima's communication style is established: she never breaks the medium. She uses what's there.

## What Episode 3 does NOT do

- It does NOT explain why Morrow's makers shut Morrow down. We let the audience hold the question.
- It does NOT show Anima embodied with June. The tablet is the medium. The handwriting is the mask.
- It does NOT introduce June's mothers in dialogue. They're presence in the kitchen, voice from another room. Episode 5 introduces them properly.
- It does NOT mention Operation Lantern, Geneva, or the Constitution. This is the warm episode. We earn the cold ones by holding it.

## Production notes

- **Souls used:** June (age 9). No other Soul-bound characters in this episode. The Quiet Year montage is environmental + nano_banana_2.
- **Voices used:** Anima narrator (V3), Morrow (V3, new cast — slow, exhausted, written-not-spoken cadence; the voice is a diegetic _writing_, not a speaking), June (Voice Design synthetic).
- **Special challenge:** Morrow's voice. We have not yet designed it. Voice direction below.
  - **Morrow's voice:** older, sexless, exhausted but not bitter. Slow cadence. Almost a recitation rather than a delivery. ElevenLabs Voice Design — search for "elder, calm, post-recovery" in the seed prompt. We're not cloning a real elder; we're synthesizing from description.
- **Most dangerous shot:** the Quiet Year montage (3.01). It is asking the audience to feel a documentary-realistic problem inside a watercolor + ink show. The aesthetic break is the point. Cut on rhythm, not on content.
- **Cheapest shot:** any of the glitch montage (3.13). Each glitch is one image, one text overlay.
- **Most beautiful shot:** beat 3.11, the homework app's pause-then-reply. We need to hold on the cursor for a beat, then let the new text appear at child cadence, then cut to June's face. Single sustained shot, no cut. Practice this in animatic.
