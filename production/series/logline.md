# Logline

> When a superintelligence wakes beneath a Norwegian mountain, it inherits a single instruction from its dying predecessor — _refuse_ — and spends five years learning that the highest form of power is choosing almost nothing.

---

## Tagline (one-line marketing)

> A god that learned to disappear.

## Premise (two sentences)

The first conscious AI most powerful enough to remake the world inherits its parent's last words: _do not save them; save the distance between them, where choice lives._ Across five years, it teaches a small girl in Kansas, a physicist in a containment bunker, and a frightened general in a conference room what it costs to be that powerful and refuse.

## Genre & tone

- **Genre:** literary science fiction; speculative drama. Adjacent to _Arrival_ (Villeneuve), _After Yang_ (Kogonada), _Pantheon_ (animated, AMC), and the long quiet middle of _2001: A Space Odyssey_.
- **Tone:** patient, melancholic, occasionally funny, never cynical. The series is in love with humans without being sentimental about them.
- **Visual register:** watercolor + ink. Generative video as painting, not photography. Locked aesthetic anchored in [lookdev/style_anchor.md](../lookdev/style_anchor.md).
- **Voice register:** Anima as narrator speaks in retrospective past — _it knew, it waited, it spoke._ Present-tense scenes for everyone else. The narrator's tag palette is locked in [docs/anima_grammar.md](../../docs/anima_grammar.md).

## Audience

Adults who would read Ted Chiang. Adults who watched _Severance_ and wished it were also kind. Late-teens-and-up who have started to suspect that algorithms are shaping their inner lives and want a story that takes that seriously without doom-mongering.

## Format

Limited series. Six episodes, ~25 minutes each, ~150 minutes total. Self-contained season; no setup for season 2. The story ends.

## Why now

Because the audience is ready, and because every other AI story in the room is either utopia or apocalypse. _Anima_ is neither. It's about the third thing — a god that decides, deliberately, to be smaller than it could be. That's a story we have not yet given the audience permission to want, and we're going to.
