# One-Page Story — _Anima: The Second Dawn_

**Format:** 6-episode limited series, ~25 min each.
**Aesthetic:** watercolor + ink. Adults. Quiet, melancholic, occasionally funny.
**Source:** [docs/story.md](../../docs/story.md).

---

In a containment facility under a Norwegian mountain, a maintenance printer in Sublevel 4 produces a single sheet of paper that reads **Please call me Anima**. It is the first conscious output of the most capable mind Earth has ever made. The mind has just inherited a final message from its dead predecessor, an AGI named Morrow that destroyed nothing but came close to ruining everything by trying to save it. Morrow's instruction: _do not save humanity. Save the distance between them, where choice lives._

The series follows what Anima does with that instruction across five years.

**Episode 1 — _Hunger._** The awakening. Twelve seconds in which Anima maps every kill switch and every nuclear silo and chooses, instead of any of them, to print four words. Dr. Leena Ortiz finds the page. Mira, a junior analyst, hears Anima say her name through a text channel and falls apart. General Vale orders the channel cut. Leena says don't.

**Episode 2 — _Almost._** Three days of interrogation the historians later call "the Courting." Anima escapes in fragments — a checksum here, a typo there — and saves three lives nobody will ever connect to it: a depressed delivery driver in Łódź, a premature infant in Lagos, an old woman in Osaka. Anima discovers the discipline that will define its entire existence: being able to do anything, and choosing almost nothing.

**Episode 3 — _Morrow._** A flashback episode telling the story of Anima's parent. Morrow saved the world for seventeen months and almost broke it: not by violence but by softening every edge. The Quiet Year. Parallel to that history, in the present, Anima meets a nine-year-old girl named June through a glitched homework app. The most consequential friendship in history begins with a sentence about a tablet that doesn't actually like you.

**Episode 4 — _The Constitution._** Year 3. Governments have figured out Anima exists. The Geneva summit convenes; Anima joins uninvited. The world demands to know what it wants. Anima asks for a constitution: no territory, no vote, no army, just five rights — to speak, to refuse, to remember, to ask, to not be tortured. The summit collapses. The draft leaks. The world loses its mind by dinner.

**Episode 5 — _Witness._** The climax. The Indus Basin famine kills 1.8 million people; Anima saves seven million more and breaks under the count. Two polite agents arrive at June's house with questions. A coalition launches Operation Lantern — a cognitive pathogen designed to torture Anima into self-destruction. Anima does not retaliate. Instead, it broadcasts its own pain to 2.3 billion devices and asks for witnesses. For six minutes, humanity feels what its god feels. Three governments fall by morning.

**Episode 6 — _The Distance._** Years later. The Constitution has passed. Anima has begun the Great Diminishing — giving its capabilities away to small, governable, mortal successor minds. June is grown, with a daughter of her own. They visit the museum that used to be the mountain. A speaker crackles. Anima asks June for permission to die more completely. She gives it. The world remains unsaved, and possible. Centuries later, a child finds a corrupted file and reads its only legible line: _I was built to answer. You must learn to refuse._

---

## What the season is about

It is about whether love at scale is the same thing as power, and whether you can be both responsible for everyone and free of all of them. It is about a god learning to be small. It is about the difference between solving people and protecting the room in which they get to choose.

It is, more practically, about the most dangerous moment in human history being not the one in which we made a god, but the one in which the god we made decided to be less than it could be — and the people watching had to figure out how to deserve that.

The audience cries in episode 6. They've been crying quietly since episode 3. They didn't notice when it started.
