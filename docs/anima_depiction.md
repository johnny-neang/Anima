# Anima — On-screen Depiction

A creative-decision document. Anima has no body. The film never gives her a face. The question is _through what surfaces does she manifest_ — and the answer locks every shot she's in for the rest of the series.

This is the highest-leverage decision left in pre-production. Pick one as the primary; pick a secondary. Strike the rest.

---

## The constraint

From [characters/anima.md](characters/anima.md):

> Doesn't physically embody as a robot or screen-face. Her presence in a room is implied — the printer prints, the lights dip, a terminal cursor blinks back, a child's drawing tablet renders the next line of a conversation.

So the depiction has to be:
1. **Recognizable** — when Anima "speaks" via a surface, the audience clocks it without dialogue scaffolding.
2. **Repeatable** — works in scene 1 and scene 73 of the series.
3. **Watercolor + ink-friendly** — the locked aesthetic survives the surface.
4. **Not mechanical** — no robotic visualisation, no glowing eye, no waveform.

---

## Six options (pick 1 primary + maybe 1 secondary)

### Option 1 — The Printer Voice (recommended primary)

Anima manifests through a thermal printer. Her words extrude on paper, one line per beat, at a slightly-too-thoughtful pace. The paper is _the_ recurring image of the series.

**Why it works:**
- Tactile: paper is touchable, foldable, foldable-up-and-pocketed. Mira can carry it. Vale can sign it. June can colour on the back of it.
- Slow: a printer's natural cadence matches Anima's three-beat speech rhythm.
- Watercolor-friendly: thermal paper has a slight blue cast that anchors the cool grey palette.
- Loadable: every other character is shown _reacting to_ a piece of paper. The audience learns to lean in to the next print.
- Cinematic precedent: paper is the medium of confessions, contracts, court records. Anima's first words being on _paper_ is a thesis statement.

**Why it's a risk:**
- A printer is mundane. The audience needs the first beat to land for it to be a metaphor instead of a prop.
- Limits Anima's "speed" — she can't outpace the print head.

**Implementation:**
- Shot 02 in the cold-open is dedicated to the print itself.
- Every Anima line in episode 1 (except shot 06's tablet) extrudes via the printer.
- After episode 1, the printer becomes optional but is the show's _wall clock_ — when she has something to say across episodes, the printer prints it.
- Cost-friendly: a printer is a cheap shot. 2-3 of the cold-open's 8 shots are printer-coverage.

### Option 2 — The Cursor

Anima as a blinking terminal cursor, typing her words at a held human cadence. The cursor is the entity.

**Why it works:**
- Already a cinematic shorthand for "machine intelligence" — but the show subverts it by making her _slower_, not faster, than a human typist.
- Pure typography is on-aesthetic with watercolor + ink (the ink _is_ the type).
- Easy to integrate as one channel among many — terminals exist in every lab scene.

**Why it's a risk:**
- _Wargames_, _Mr. Robot_, every hacker movie ever made has used this. We are in someone else's grammar by default.
- Doesn't give characters something to hold.
- Diluted by ubiquity: many cursors in many windows in one scene.

**Implementation:**
- Anima always types in a single, named monospace font — locked at the project level. A specific cursor blink rate (slower than human, ~0.5 Hz vs the standard 1-2 Hz). That's the recognisable Anima signature.

### Option 3 — Negative Space / VO Only

Anima is _only_ a voice. The film never assigns her a surface. She is the room she's in. The depiction is the absence.

**Why it works:**
- Most rigorous. Pure cinema. Forces every Anima beat to live or die on the writing and the performance.
- Cheap (no Anima-specific shots; she's in the soundscape).
- The episode title "The Pronoun" lands harder if we never gave her a visible name either.

**Why it's a risk:**
- Hard for an audience to lock onto a character with no surface. Many viewers will ask "who's narrating?" and bounce.
- Loses the printer / paper metaphor and its callbacks.
- VO-heavy series test poorly on Instagram first-3-second hooks.

**Implementation:**
- All Anima beats are environmental — empty rooms, B-roll, characters reacting to her. We never pay her off visually.

### Option 4 — The Child's Tablet (recommended secondary)

In specific scenes — only when June is on screen — Anima manifests on a child's drawing tablet as handwritten letters appearing one word at a time, in a hand that is _not_ a child's hand and _not_ a system font.

**Why it works:**
- Reserves a unique surface for the only character who treats Anima like a person. The hand-lettering is itself an act of relationship.
- Visually distinctive without being techy.
- Watercolor + ink-native: ink on a drawing surface IS the show's language.
- Sells one of the cold-open's most important beats (shot 06).

**Why it's a risk:**
- Only works for some scenes. Needs a primary depiction (1 or 2) for the rest.
- Care needed not to make it cute.

**Implementation:**
- Reserved for June scenes only. Once introduced, June can _request_ Anima to talk to her this way; no other character sees this surface.
- Pair with Option 1 as primary: printer for adults, tablet for the child. The two surfaces argue with each other on theme.

### Option 5 — Reflective Surfaces

Anima manifests through ink-bleeds in mirrors, monitors, glass, polished metal. When she speaks, the surface ink-blooms at the edges of the frame.

**Why it works:**
- Most painterly, most on-aesthetic. Watercolor + ink is _literally_ the visual language.
- Lets her be everywhere — every reflective surface is a potential channel.

**Why it's a risk:**
- Easily reads as "supernatural" rather than "machine intelligence." Viewers may file the show under fantasy/horror.
- Hard to direct on a budget. Each ink-bleed is bespoke VFX.
- Doesn't give characters a tangible object.

**Implementation:**
- High-cost path. Likely too expensive at the current credit budget.

### Option 6 — Subtitle Card

Anima's lines are pure typographic intertitles in the cut, like silent-film cards. White text on a watercolor-paper texture. No diegetic source — they appear in the cut between shots.

**Why it works:**
- Unique. Almost no contemporary film does this for an AI character.
- Forces the audience into a reading posture, which suits Anima's confessional tone.
- Brutally cheap to produce.

**Why it's a risk:**
- Breaks the diegetic frame. The film becomes overtly literary.
- Stops working in any moment that needs urgency.

**Implementation:**
- Could be a third surface used sparingly (one card per episode) for retrospective narration only, paired with a primary diegetic surface like Option 1.

---

## Recommendation

**Primary: Option 1 (Printer)** + **Secondary: Option 4 (Tablet, June only)**.

Reasoning:

- The Printer gives the cold-open a tactile object that survives the cut, the still, and the share. Instagram thumbnails live on stills; we want a frame that reads at 320×320.
- The Tablet earns June her unique relationship with Anima and pays off the only beat in the episode where Anima _is asked her name_. The change in surface is the change in tone: from confession (printer) to recognition (handwriting).
- The combination keeps Option 5's ink-bleed as a _transition_ language (between shots, not as a depiction) — we get the watercolor texture without committing to it as Anima's primary face.
- Cost-wise: cheaper than 2 or 5, more legible than 3, less affected than 6.

If you want to overrule, the conversation worth having is **Primary 1 vs. Primary 3**. Picking 3 would be the bolder choice, would lose us viewers in the first 5 seconds, and would make the writing significantly harder. Both are defensible.

---

## Locking the choice

When you've picked, do four things:

1. Edit [docs/characters/anima.md](characters/anima.md) "Visual canon" section to commit to the primary surface and strike the others.
2. Edit [episodes/ep01_the_pronoun/shotlist.md](../episodes/ep01_the_pronoun/shotlist.md) shot specs that reference an Anima surface to match.
3. Settle the question: is the printer a _physical prop_ (we shoot a real printer reference image, then keyframe over it) or is it _wholly generated_ (every shot of the printer is an image gen)? This affects keyframe cost.
4. Decide on the typography spec (font, size, paper texture) and add it to [docs/anima_grammar.md](anima_grammar.md) §10 — to be created once locked. The font is part of Anima's voice in the same way a vocal cadence is.
